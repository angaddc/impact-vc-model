The Impact-Hurdle Carry Model

Some impact funds pay their team's carry only if the portfolio hits its impact targets first and the fund makes money second. This model plays out 20,000 possible funds of 30 start-ups each and asks how often a carry structure like Rubio Impact Ventures' Aligned Impact Model actually pays out.

How it works: each company's money outcome comes from the Stochastic VC Method (failure as a weighted coin flip, lognormal revenue and exit multiple, dilution per round), with a shared market shock so funds have good and bad vintages. Each company has 1–3 impact KPIs scored as actual ÷ target. The scores move with the company's revenue shock by the impact link and are cut if the company fails or its KPI turns out wrong. The fund score is the cost-weighted average, as in the European Investment Fund method Rubio's model comes from. Carry is 20% of profit over paid-in capital (no preferred return), then multiplied by the two gates.

Rubio's carry terms are only partly public: it publishes the 100% impact link, the 60% fund hurdle (80–100% on its main site), the 2× net money hurdle and that forfeited carry goes to charity, but not its carry rate, waterfall or whether the gate is all-or-nothing. Those are set here as adjustable assumptions. Sources: Rubio, Aligned Impact Model; Rubio impact methodology; Impact Accountability in VC (EIF Gamma Model). Illustrative only, not investment advice.
